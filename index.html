03 · The Singapore Canvas

Asia's
Pinnacle
of Finance

Singapore is not merely a location; it is the pinnacle of global finance. Ranked the world's most competitive economy by the IMD World Competitiveness Yearbook 2024, it is home to over 7,000 MNCs, three global stock exchanges, and Asia's fastest-growing family office ecosystem — over 1,650 single-family offices registered as of 2024.

For the Global Indian Chartered Accountant, Singapore represents the ultimate convergence of heritage and high performance. Our members sit in CFO suites at DBS, Standard Chartered and HSBC. They run compliance at MAS-regulated fintechs, structure cross-border investments along the India–Singapore corridor, and increasingly, they build their own firms. Since April 2009, the ICAI Singapore Chapter has been the professional home of this community — 500-strong, award-winning, and irreversibly woven into the Lion City's financial fabric.

🏦

04 · The Global Indian Identity

Professionalism as a
Cultural Export

The Indian CA has become Singapore's most trusted financial professional.
The Luminance Argument

In Singapore's hyper-competitive professional landscape, the Indian CA is not just a technical resource; they are an institution builder. From humble beginnings as expatriate finance managers, the community has evolved into founders of advisory firms, CFOs of listed entities, and board directors of sovereign wealth-backed institutions.

The ICAI qualification — rigorous, globally benchmarked, and culturally infused with a discipline of clarity — has become Singapore's most respected foreign professional credential in finance. It signals not merely competence, but character.

As Singapore cements its stature as Asia's most sophisticated financial hub, the Global Indian CA stands at the nexus of two of the world's most dynamic economies — India and Singapore — translating opportunity in both directions. The ICAI Singapore Chapter is the home base for this extraordinary tribe.

UAE–Singapore Synergy

A growing number of ICAI CAs now bridge UAE and Singapore markets — leveraging the tax treaty, India-Singapore DTAA, and free trade agreements to structure cross-border investment, family office migrations, and holding company frameworks. This corridor is the defining opportunity of the decade.

09 · Thought Leadership 2026

Insight for the
Discerning Professional

Cover Story · IFRS
IFRS 17 at Full Sail: What Singapore's Insurers Must Act On Now
With IFRS 17 Insurance Contracts now fully operative across Singapore's insurance sector, finance leaders are navigating a transformed reporting landscape. The Contractual Service Margin — a metric virtually unknown five years ago — now sits at the heart of profitability analysis, dividend signalling, and investor communication.

For Indian CAs working within Singapore's insurance industry, this represents both a challenge and an exceptional opportunity. The technical rigour demanded by IFRS 17's three measurement models requires professionals with deep actuarial-accounting fluency — precisely the profile that ICAI training cultivates.
CA Nikhil Jain · Secretary, ICAI-SG
IFRS 17
Tax Intelligence
Pillar Two & Singapore: Navigating the Global Minimum Tax
The OECD's Pillar Two global minimum tax — a 15% floor on the effective tax rate of large MNCs — came into force for Singapore in 2025 via the Income Inclusion Rule and QDMTT. For the thousands of MNCs using Singapore as their APAC headquarters or IP holding location, the comfort of the 17% headline rate now requires careful architecture. CAs advising on transfer pricing and holding structure design must factor GloBE income calculations into every engagement.
CA Ayush Yagnik
Pillar Two
Technology
The AI Audit: How Generative AI is Redefining Assurance
Deloitte's Omnia AI, KPMG Clara, and EY's EYQ platform are now deployed in Singapore audit engagements. Generative AI is compressing substantive testing timelines and enabling continuous monitoring at a granularity previously impossible. The CA's role is shifting from data gatherer to professional sceptic and AI interpreter — a higher-value, higher-stakes position that demands new literacy alongside time-tested judgement.
CA Vishal Tibrewala
AI · Audit
UAE–Singapore
The Corridor Opportunity: India–Singapore–UAE
The triangular corridor connecting India, Singapore, and the UAE has emerged as the defining structuring opportunity for Indian CAs in 2026. Singapore's DTAA with India, the UAE's zero corporate tax regime, and Singapore's Variable Capital Company structure together create a powerful toolkit for family offices, fund managers, and MNCs looking to optimise across jurisdictions. The CA who understands all three regimes commands a premium at the table.
CA Anshu Jain
UAE–SG
ESG Reporting
ISSB Standards: Singapore's Mandatory Climate Disclosure Journey
SGX RegCo's phased mandatory climate disclosure regime — aligned with IFRS S1 and S2 — places Singapore among the first markets in Asia to require ISSB-standard reporting from listed issuers. For CAs in the preparer role, understanding Scope 3 measurement, climate scenario analysis, and the nuances of transition risk disclosure is no longer optional. The finance function now owns the sustainability narrative.
CA Sarita Agrawal
ISSB · ESG

Composite Portrait · A Member Story

"The flight from Chennai to Singapore takes four and a half hours. But the journey takes a lifetime."

Rajan came to Singapore in 2009 with a duffel bag, a CA qualification, and the phone number of a college senior who'd promised to help him find his feet. He found a one-room HDB rental in Bedok, a job at a mid-tier audit firm in Raffles Place, and on his second weekend in the city, someone told him there was an ICAI Singapore event happening at a function room in Little India.
He went alone. He knew nobody. By the end of the evening, he had three business cards, two dinner invitations, and one offer to shadow a CFO for a day who worked at a listed company three floors above his own office. That CFO became his mentor. Twelve years later, Rajan is the CFO.
"Singapore can be a lonely city when you first arrive. The ICAI chapter was the first place that felt like it already knew who I was — before I even introduced myself."
The years between 2009 and today were not a straight line. There was the year he got passed over for a promotion he deserved, and the chapter chairman at the time — someone he barely knew — took him for coffee at Maxwell Food Centre and said, simply: "You are better than this company knows yet. Give it one more year."
He gave it one more year. Then two. Then a decade and a half.

His daughter was born in Singapore General Hospital on a Tuesday in March 2013. His wife, Priya, also a CA, worked until the week before. They had, by then, stopped counting themselves as expats. Singapore was home. The ICAI chapter was part of that home — Diwali celebrations where their daughter learnt the names of sweets, Republic Day gatherings where she stood for both national anthems with equal confidence, chapter networking evenings where she fell asleep on a chair in the corner of the room surrounded by the sound of adults talking about IFRS and tax and interest rates.
She is twelve now. She wants to be a doctor. Rajan says that is absolutely fine. Priya suspects she will eventually end up in finance anyway. "It's in the air at home," she says.
"My daughter grew up at ICAI events. She doesn't know any other kind of community. That is the greatest thing the chapter ever gave me — not a job, not a contact. A sense of belonging that my child could inherit."

Rajan serves on the chapter's technical committee now. He takes the newer members for coffee in the way that CFO once took him. He knows that this is how it works — not policies, not programmes, but people reaching back to steady the next one coming up the escalator. On his desk at work is a small framed note his daughter made when she was six, with a felt-tip drawing of a building and the words: "THIS IS DADDY'S WORK." She did not know, at six, that the building she drew looks exactly like the Marina Bay skyline.
She drew it anyway.
